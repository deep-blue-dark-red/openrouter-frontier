\title{A Task-Level Cost Model for LLM Endpoint Selection\\
\large with prompt caching, ordered fallbacks, time value, and process and parameter risk}
\author{Matthew Pollard}
\date{2026-09-04}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The price of a language-model call is not its list price. Over a multi-turn task
the transcript is resubmitted at every turn, so the caller pays for a growing
prefix $N$ times. Whether that prefix is read from a prompt cache, rewritten into
it, or processed cold; whether the request fails and is served by a fallback; and
how long the caller waits all change the bill, and all of them differ between
providers serving the same model. This document defines the cost of a
\emph{task} as the sum of its turn costs, derives its expectation in closed form
under an explicit routing policy, separates process variance (realised misses)
from parameter variance (uncertainty in the hit rate itself, which scales as
$N^4$), and states how each input maps to a field OpenRouter publishes or to a
caller assumption.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

\subsection{Task profile}

A task is a sequence of $N$ turns indexed $k = 1, \dots, N$. At each turn the
caller appends $a$ new prompt tokens (user text or tool results) and the model
emits $o$ completion tokens, so the transcript grows by $d = a + o$ tokens per
turn. At turn $k$ the prompt is the full transcript so far: a \emph{reusable
prefix}
\[
  S_k = (k-1)\,d, \qquad k = 1,\dots,N,
\]
that was already sent on the previous turn, followed by the $a$ new tokens,
which no cache can have seen. The total prompt is $P_k = S_k + a$. Inter-turn
gaps are assumed short relative to cache retention; Section~\ref{sec:beyond}
relaxes this.

\subsection{Endpoint prices: three prompt prices, not two}\label{sec:prices}

An endpoint $e$ bills prompt tokens in three categories, each with its own
per-token price (USD per token; the tools display per million):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Category & OpenRouter price field & Usage field \\
\midrule
$b_e$ & ordinary uncached input & \texttt{prompt} & --- \\
$r_e$ & cache read & \texttt{input\_cache\_read} & \texttt{cached\_tokens} \\
$w_e$ & cache write & \texttt{input\_cache\_write} & \texttt{cache\_write\_tokens} \\
$c_e$ & completion & \texttt{completion} & \texttt{completion\_tokens} \\
$f_e$ & fixed fee per request (USD) & \texttt{request} & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Providers differ in how they price writes: some charge a premium
($w_e = 1.25\,b_e$ or $2\,b_e$), some charge nothing beyond ordinary input
($w_e = b_e$), and some charge nothing at all for writes. When no write price
is published we set $w_e = b_e$. When no read price is published the endpoint
has no cache and $r_e = w_e = b_e$, $h_e = 0$. Prices from the endpoints API
are already net of any advertised discount.

The billing primitive is a partition of the prompt into ordinary, read, and
written tokens, $U_k + C_k + W_k = P_k$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
  I_k = f + b\,U_k + r\,C_k + w\,W_k .
\end{equation}
Everything below is a policy for how $(U_k, C_k, W_k)$ is generated.

\subsection{Endpoint telemetry}

OpenRouter publishes, per endpoint:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Window & Source \\
\midrule

$\ell^{50}_e, \ell^{90}_e$ & time to first token, s & 5 min rolling & \texttt{p50\_latency}, \texttt{p90\_latency} \\
$g^{50}_e, g^{90}_e$ & output throughput, tokens/s & 5 min rolling & \texttt{p50\_throughput}, \texttt{p90\_throughput} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Two percentile conventions are in play. OpenRouter's routing documentation
defines a p90 threshold as a value that 90\% of requests \emph{beat}. The
model-page statistics the tools read, however, are plain distribution
percentiles: $\ell^{90} > \ell^{50}$ is the slow latency tail and
$g^{90} > g^{50}$ is the \emph{fast} throughput tail (for GLM 5.3 Flash on
2026-09-04, $g^{50} = 23.5$ and $g^{90} = 47$ tokens/s on one endpoint). The
fits in Section~\ref{sec:inputs} use the absolute log-ratio of the two
quantiles, which is the same under either convention. The percentiles summarise
a five-minute window while the rates summarise a day; the model treats both as
current.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Whole-prefix caching]\label{as:policy}
On a successful turn the cached fraction $H_k \in [0,1]$ of the reusable prefix
is read at $r$. The remaining $(1 - H_k) S_k$ prefix tokens are billed at the
miss price $m \in \{w, b\}$: $m = w$ if the provider rewrites the prefix into the
cache on a miss (the default), $m = b$ if it merely processes it uncached. The
$a$ new tokens are appended to the cache and billed at $w$. In the notation of
\eqref{eq:partition}: $C_k = H_k S_k$, $W_k = a + (1-H_k) S_k$ under rewriting,
or $W_k = a,\ U_k = (1-H_k) S_k$ under processing.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent cache fractions]\label{as:indep}
The $H_k$ are independent across turns with common mean $\E[H_k] = h$. The
Bernoulli case $H_k \in \{0,1\}$, $\Prob[H_k = 0] = 1 - h$, is the member of this
family with the largest variance, $\Var[H_k] = h(1-h)$; it is used only for
bounds.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent failures and an ordered fallback chain]\label{as:fail}
A request to endpoint $e$ fails with probability $q_e = 1 - u_e$,
independently of everything else, and a failed request is not billed for
tokens. A turn is attempted along the chain $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_m$ until one
succeeds. The last link is an outside option with $u_{e_m} = 1$ (or the task
is declared failed with the residual probability). Fallback endpoints hold no
cached prefix for this task unless the routing policy says otherwise.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Time is linear in tokens]\label{as:time}
A request costs $\ell$ seconds to first token plus the prefill of any prompt
tokens the cache does not hold, and $o \cdot s$ seconds to generate, where
$s = 1/g$ is seconds per output token. Prompt processing runs at
$g_p = \gamma\, g$ tokens per second, roughly a hundred times decode, so a
missed prefix of $S_k$ tokens adds $S_k / g_p$ seconds. The published time to
first token is taken to cover queueing and the prefill of the new tokens $a$
under the publisher's typical cache mix; only the missed prefix is charged on
top of it. Time is valued at $v$ USD per hour; write $v' = v / 3600$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Missing telemetry is worst-case]\label{as:missing}
If an endpoint lacks a latency, throughput, or uptime observation, the value is
imputed as the worst observed among endpoints serving the same model. If a
cacheable endpoint has no published hit rate it is scored cold, $h = 0$. An
endpoint is never made cheaper by having less data.
\end{assumption}

\section{Inputs}\label{sec:inputs}

\subsection{Hit rate: the published number is a proxy with the wrong denominator}

The published $h^{\text{obs}}$ is cached tokens over \emph{all} prompt tokens
in the endpoint's 24-hour traffic. The model needs the cached fraction of the
\emph{reusable prefix}. Under Assumption~\ref{as:policy} these differ by the
share of new tokens in the prompt: for a turn with prefix $S$ and $a$ new
tokens, cached tokens are $H S$ and prompt tokens are $S + a$, so
\[
  h^{\text{obs}} = h \cdot \frac{S}{S + a} \;\le\; h .
\]
The conversion factor depends on the \emph{publisher's} traffic mix, which is
not observable, so $h^{\text{obs}}$ cannot be inverted exactly. It is a
conservative proxy: it understates the reusable-prefix hit rate by the new-token
share of whatever workloads that endpoint served. The tools therefore expose
four cache modes: \textsf{aggregate} uses $h = h^{\text{obs}}$ as-is;
\textsf{cold} sets $h = 0$ for a bound; \textsf{assumed} takes a caller-supplied
$h$; \textsf{trace} estimates $h$ from the caller's own usage records, which
report \texttt{cached\_tokens} and \texttt{cache\_write\_tokens} per request.

No shrinkage toward a prior is applied. The published rate is an average over
tens of thousands to millions of requests (the smallest primary GLM 5.3 Flash
endpoint served $3 \times 10^8$ tokens on 2026-09-04, the largest
$4 \times 10^{10}$), so its sampling error is below $0.3$ percentage points for
every endpoint. Any prior heavy enough to move it would penalise smaller
providers for being smaller, not for being uncertain. The uncertainty that
matters is epistemic: the window drifts day to day, and the workload mix is not
the caller's. That enters as $\sigma_h$ in Section~\ref{sec:param}.

\subsection{Uptime}

$u$ is OpenRouter's request-weighted success fraction over 24 hours and is used
as published. Token volume is not a sample size for uptime and is not used as
one.

\subsection{Latency and throughput: expected time from two quantiles}

The time cost needs expectations, and a blend of two quantiles is not one. Fit
a lognormal to each: with median $\ell^{50}$ and 90th percentile $\ell^{90}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lognormal-l}
  \sigma_\ell = \frac{\ln(\ell^{90} / \ell^{50})}{\Phi^{-1}(0.9)},
  \qquad
  \E[\ell] = \ell^{50} \exp\!\big(\sigma_\ell^2 / 2\big),
  \qquad \Phi^{-1}(0.9) = 1.2816 .
\end{equation}
Generation time is linear in seconds per token $s = 1/g$, not in $g$, so the
fit is on $s$. The published throughput quantile may be the fast or the slow
tail depending on the source (Section~1.3); the lognormal is symmetric in
$\ln s$, so either decile identifies $\sigma_s$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lognormal-s}
  \sigma_s = \frac{\big|\ln(g^{90} / g^{50})\big|}{\Phi^{-1}(0.9)},
  \qquad
  \E[s] = \frac{1}{g^{50}} \exp\!\big(\sigma_s^2 / 2\big).
\end{equation}
Latency is treated the same way, $\sigma_\ell = |\ln(\ell^{90}/\ell^{50})| / \Phi^{-1}(0.9)$.
When only a median is published, $\sigma = 0$ and the median is used. Both
expectations are increasing in the tail width, so a wider tail can only make an
endpoint look worse.

\section{Turn cost}

Fix the scored endpoint $A = e_1$ with prices $b, r, w, c, f$, miss price $m$,
inputs $h, u, \E[\ell], \E[s]$, and $q = 1 - u$.

\subsection{Successful turn}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-ok}
  X_k^{\text{ok}}
  = \underbrace{f + w\,a + c\,o}_{\text{fixed}}
  + \underbrace{v'\big(\ell + o\, s\big)}_{\text{time}}
  + \underbrace{S_k\,\big[\, m - H_k\,(m - r)\,\big]}_{\text{prefix money}}
  + \underbrace{v'\,(1 - H_k)\, S_k / g_p}_{\text{prefix re-prefill}} .
\end{equation}
Taking expectations, and writing $\rho = v' / g_p$ for the time value of
re-prefilling one prefix token,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-ok-mean}
  \mu_k^{\text{ok}} = \alpha + \pi\, S_k,
  \qquad
  \alpha = f + w\,a + c\,o + v'\big(\E[\ell] + o\,\E[s]\big),
  \qquad
  \pi = h\, r + (1 - h)\,(m + \rho) .
\end{equation}
$\pi$ is the \emph{effective prefix price}, in dollars of money and time per
prefix token. The new tokens never receive it. A cache miss therefore costs
both the price spread $m - r$ and $\rho$ per token of waiting; on a 200k-token
prefix at $g_p = 3000$ tokens/s the wait alone is over a minute.

\subsection{Failed turn on a two-link chain}

Take the chain $(A, B)$ with $u_B = 1$; the general chain is in
Section~\ref{sec:chain}. With probability $q$ the request to $A$ fails. The
caller has waited $\E[\ell_A]$ for nothing, then the turn is served cold by
$B$: every prompt token is new to $B$ and is written at $w_B$.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-fail}
\begin{split}
  X_k^{\text{fail}}
  = v' \E[\ell_A]
  &+ \underbrace{f_B + w_B\,(S_k + a) + c_B\, o + v'\big(\E[\ell_B] + o\,\E[s_B]\big) + \rho_B\, S_k}_{\text{cold turn on } B} \\
  &+ \underbrace{\ind[\textsf{order}]\;\ind[k < N]\;h\,(m - r + \rho)\, d}_{\text{return penalty}} .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This branch is deterministic given the failure, so
$\mu_k^{\text{fail}} = \beta + (w_B + \rho_B) S_k + \ind[\textsf{order}]\ind[k<N]\,h(m-r+\rho)d$
with $\beta = v'\E[\ell_A] + f_B + w_B a + c_B o + v'(\E[\ell_B] + o\E[s_B])$.

\paragraph{The return penalty depends on the routing policy.}
Under an explicit provider \textsf{order}, OpenRouter disables load balancing
and the next turn goes back to $A$. $A$ never saw turn $k$'s completion, so of
$S_{k+1}$ only $S_k$ can be cached and the extra $d$ tokens are written at $w$
rather than read at the blended price, and re-prefilled; the expected extra cost is $h(m-r+\rho)d$,
booked here at turn $k$ (there is no turn $N+1$, hence the indicator). Under
\textsf{sticky} routing, OpenRouter's default, the provider that served the last
cached request becomes the sticky provider, so after a fallback the task
\emph{stays on $B$}: there is no return penalty, but every subsequent turn is
billed at $B$'s prices and hit rate. That is a regime change, not a per-turn
surcharge, and it has its own closed form in Section~\ref{sec:sticky}.

\subsection{Expected turn cost under \textsf{order}}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-mean}
\begin{split}
  \E[X_k] &= (1-q)\,\mu_k^{\text{ok}} + q\,\mu_k^{\text{fail}} \\
  &= \underbrace{(1-q)\,\alpha + q\,\beta}_{A_0}
  + \underbrace{\big[(1-q)\,\pi + q\,(w_B + \rho_B)\big]}_{\Pi}\, S_k
  + q\,\ind[k<N]\,h\,(m-r+\rho)\,d .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Apart from the last-turn indicator the turn cost is affine in the prefix, with
intercept $A_0$ and slope $\Pi$, the failure-adjusted effective prefix price.

\section{Task cost}

\begin{proposition}[Task cost under \textsf{order}]\label{prop:task}
The task cost is the sum of the turn costs, $X_{\text{task}} = \sum_{k=1}^N X_k$.
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:policy}--\ref{as:time}, the two-link chain, routing
policy \textsf{order}, and constant growth $S_k = (k-1)d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:task-mean}
  \boxed{\;
  \E[X_{\text{task}}]
  = N\, A_0 \;+\; \Pi\, d\, \frac{N(N-1)}{2} \;+\; q\,h\,(m-r+\rho)\,d\,(N-1)
  \;}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sum \eqref{eq:turn-mean} over $k$ using $\sum_{k=1}^{N}(k-1) = N(N-1)/2$ and
$\sum_{k=1}^{N}\ind[k<N] = N-1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Decomposition}

Expanding $\Pi$ separates what the caller pays under a perfect cache from what
misses and failures add:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
\begin{split}
  \Pi\, d\, \frac{N(N-1)}{2}
  = \underbrace{r\, d\, \tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}}_{\text{cached-read baseline}}
  &+ \underbrace{(1-q)(1-h)(m - r + \rho)\, d\, \tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}}_{\text{miss premium}} \\
  &+ \underbrace{q\,(w_B + \rho_B - r)\, d\, \tfrac{N(N-1)}{2}}_{\text{failure premium}} .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
All three are $\Theta(N^2)$: there are $N$ submissions and the $k$-th exposes a
prefix of $(k-1)d$ tokens, so every per-token surcharge on the prefix is paid on
a quadratically growing volume. A hit rate of 95\% is not a 5\% surcharge on a
long task; the miss premium is $\Theta((1-h)N^2)$ while $N A_0$ does not grow
with context at all. The return-penalty term is linear in $N$ and $O(q)$.

\begin{remark}[Mean-prefix equivalence]
Ignoring the $O(q)$ return term, \eqref{eq:turn-mean} is affine in $S_k$, so
$\E[X_{\text{task}}] = N \cdot \E[X_k \mid S_k = \bar S]$ with
$\bar S = d(N-1)/2$. The task equals $N$ representative turns at the mean
prefix, not one. The factor $N$ does not affect the ranking of endpoints but
does affect totals.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the single-turn scorer]
The current scorer takes one prompt size $C$ and applies $\pi$ to all of it.
That treats the new tokens as partly cached and underprices each turn by
$h\,(m - r)\,a$; it uses two prompt prices where there are three; it charges no time for re-prefilling a missed prefix; and it reports
one turn, not $N$. Replacing $C$ by the pair $(a, S_k)$, adding the write price,
and summing over $k$ recovers \eqref{eq:task-mean}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Comparing endpoints}

For endpoints $A$ and $A'$ serving the same model,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compare}
  \E[X^{A}_{\text{task}}] - \E[X^{A'}_{\text{task}}]
  = N\,\big(A_0 - A_0'\big) + \big(\Pi - \Pi'\big)\, d\, \frac{N(N-1)}{2}
  + O(q)\,(N-1).
\end{equation}
$A_0 - A_0'$ contains price differences, time-value differences, and $O(q)$
failure terms, so it does not vanish in general. When the two endpoints have
identical prices and $v = 0$ it is $O(q)$, and the gap is then dominated by the
quadratic term, which is driven by $h$ and $u$.

\section{General fallback chain}\label{sec:chain}

For the chain $e_1, \dots, e_m$, let $R_j = \prod_{i<j} q_{e_i}$ be the
probability that attempt $j$ is reached. Each reached attempt succeeds with
probability $u_{e_j}$ at cost $\mu^{S}_{j,k}$ (warm on $e_1$, cold on any
$e_j$, $j \ge 2$) or fails at cost $\mu^{F}_{j,k} = v'\E[\ell_{e_j}]$ (the
wasted wait). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
  \E[X_k] = \sum_{j=1}^{m} R_j \Big[ u_{e_j}\,\mu^{S}_{j,k} + (1 - u_{e_j})\,\mu^{F}_{j,k} \Big]
  + R_{m+1}\, \Xi ,
\end{equation}
where $R_{m+1}$ is the probability every link fails and $\Xi$ is the cost
assigned to a failed task (or $R_{m+1} = 0$ if $u_{e_m} = 1$). The two-link
formula \eqref{eq:turn-mean} is the case $m = 2$, $u_B = 1$; using a fallible
$B$ instead changes the expectation by $O(q_A q_B)$. Setting $B = A$ is a
proxy for ``retried later, cold'' and should not be read as the same endpoint
succeeding with certainty after failing.

\section{Sticky routing: failures migrate the task}\label{sec:sticky}

Under \textsf{sticky} routing with two endpoints, the task runs warm on $A$
until $A$'s first failure, is served cold by $B$ on that turn, and then runs
warm on $B$ for the rest of the task. Let $x = 1 - q$. The probability the
task is still on $A$ at turn $k$ is $x^{k-1}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sticky}
  \E[X_{\text{task}}]
  = \sum_{k=1}^{N} x^{k-1}\Big[ x\,(\alpha + \pi S_k) + q\,(\beta + w_B S_k) \Big]
  + \sum_{k=1}^{N} \big(1 - x^{k-1}\big)\,\big(\alpha_B + \pi_B S_k\big),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_B, \pi_B$ are $B$'s warm intercept and effective prefix price.
With $G_0(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} x^{k-1} = (1-x^N)/(1-x)$ and
$G_1(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} (k-1)x^{k-1} = x\big(1 - N x^{N-1} + (N-1)x^N\big)/(1-x)^2$,
\[
  \E[X_{\text{task}}]
  = \big(x\alpha + q\beta - \alpha_B\big) G_0(x)
  + \big(x\pi + q w_B - \pi_B\big)\, d\, G_1(x)
  + N \alpha_B + \pi_B\, d\, \frac{N(N-1)}{2}.
\]
The probability the task ends on $B$ is $1 - (1-q)^{N}$. For $N = 200$ this is
63\% at $u = 0.995$ and 89\% at $u = 0.989$: under the default routing policy
a long task on any endpoint short of near-perfect uptime will more likely than
not finish on its fallback, and it is the fallback's hit rate and prices that
govern most of the quadratic term. Ranking endpoints by their own $h$ alone is
therefore only valid under \textsf{order}, or when $qN \ll 1$.

\section{Variance and risk}\label{sec:var}

Two distinct kinds of variance apply, and they scale differently in $N$.

\subsection{Process variance: realised misses and failures}

Under Assumptions~\ref{as:indep} and \ref{as:fail} with routing \textsf{order},
$X_k$ depends only on $(H_k, F_k)$ where $F_k$ is the failure indicator, so the
turns are independent and $\Var[X_{\text{task}}] = \sum_k \Var[X_k]$. By the law
of total variance,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var-turn}
  \Var[X_k] = (1-q)\, V_k^{\text{ok}} + q\, V_k^{\text{fail}}
  + q(1-q)\,\big(\mu_k^{\text{fail}} - \mu_k^{\text{ok}}\big)^2,
\end{equation}
with $V_k^{\text{fail}} = 0$ (the cold branch is deterministic) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var-ok}
  V_k^{\text{ok}} = (m-r+\rho)^2\, S_k^2\, \Var[H_k] \;\le\; (m-r+\rho)^2\, h(1-h)\, S_k^2 .
\end{equation}
Summing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var}
\begin{split}
  \Var[X_{\text{task}}]
  \;\le\;& (1-q)\,(m-r+\rho)^2\, h(1-h)\, d^2\, \frac{(N-1)N(2N-1)}{6} \\
  &+ q(1-q) \sum_{k=1}^{N} \big[(\beta - \alpha) + (w_B + \rho_B - \pi) S_k + \ind[k<N]\,h\,(m-r+\rho)\,d\big]^2 .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The first term is attained by the Bernoulli model; its standard deviation grows
as $N^{3/2}$. Because the published $h$ identifies only $\E[H_k]$, this is an
upper bound on process risk, not an estimate of it, and no normal-tail
statement should be made from it.

\begin{remark}[Bookkeeping of the return penalty]
Equation \eqref{eq:turn-fail} books the return penalty at turn $k$ as an
expected quantity. If instead the realised extra cost is attributed to turn
$k+1$, $X_{k+1}$ depends on $F_k$ and the turns are no longer independent;
$\Cov(X_k, X_{k+1}) = O(q)\,(m-r+\rho)\,d\,S_{k+1}$ terms appear. The expectation is
unchanged either way. The model as written is exact for its own bookkeeping and
neglects covariance of order $q$ under the alternative.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Parameter variance: the hit rate itself is uncertain}\label{sec:param}

Suppose the true reusable-prefix hit rate for this task is $h + \delta$, with
$\E[\delta] = 0$ and $\Var[\delta] = \sigma_h^2$, shared across all turns
(drift of the 24-hour window, or mismatch between the published workload and
the caller's). Since $\partial \E[X_{\text{task}}] / \partial h =
-(1-q)(m-r+\rho)\sum_k S_k + O(q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:var-param}
  \sigma_{\text{par}} = (1-q)\,(m-r+\rho)\,\sigma_h\, d\,\frac{N(N-1)}{2} = O(N^2),
  \qquad
  \Var_{\text{par}} = O(N^4).
\end{equation}
A shared error in $h$ is paid on every prefix token of every turn, so its
standard deviation grows as $N^2$ and eventually dominates the $N^{3/2}$ process
term. For a task long enough that cache economics matter, being wrong about
$h$ by a few points is a larger risk than any realised run of misses.
$\sigma_h$ is a caller input: the observed day-to-day standard deviation of
the published rate once history is recorded, or a stated band for workload
mismatch. It is not derived from token volume.

\subsection{Decision rule}

Given a quality constraint $Q_m \ge q_{\min}$ on the model $m$ served, choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decision}
  \min_{(m, e)} \;\;
  J(m, e) = \E[X_{\text{task}}] + \lambda_{\text{proc}}\, \sigma_{\text{proc}} + \lambda_{\text{par}}\, \sigma_{\text{par}}
  \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Q_m \ge q_{\min},
\end{equation}
with both $\lambda$ zero for a risk-neutral caller. $\lambda_{\text{proc}}$
prices operational risk (a bad run of misses); $\lambda_{\text{par}}$ prices
epistemic risk (the input was wrong). The Pareto tools report the non-dominated
set over
\[
  \big(\E[X_{\text{task}}] \downarrow,\ \E[\ell] \downarrow,\ g^{50} \uparrow,\ h \uparrow,\ u \uparrow\big)
\]
so that a caller who will not commit to $v$ and the $\lambda$s can still see
the trade-offs.

\section{Worked example}

GLM 5.3 Flash, primary endpoints, 2026-09-04. Every endpoint lists
$b = 0.075$, $r = 0.015$, $c = 0.25$ USD per million tokens and publishes no
write price, so $w = m = b$ and only telemetry separates them. Task profile
$a = 2000$, $o = 500$, $N = 200$, $v = 0$ (so $\rho = 0$), routing
\textsf{order}, chain $(A, A\text{ cold})$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Endpoint & $h$ & $u$ & $\Pi$ (\$/M) & $\E[X_{\text{task}}]$ \\
\midrule
Z.ai      & 0.885 & 0.989 & 0.0225 & \$1.17 \\
DeepInfra & 0.469 & 0.995 & 0.0470 & \$2.39 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

With $\sum_k S_k = d\,N(N-1)/2 = 49.75$M prefix tokens, the quadratic term is
\$1.12 on Z.ai and \$2.34 on DeepInfra, against a linear term of \$0.055 on
both and a return-penalty term below \$0.001. At the default single turn of the
current tools the two endpoints differ by 29\%; over the task they differ by
104\%, almost all of it from the hit rate, with uptime moving $\Pi$ in the
third decimal. The process bound \eqref{eq:var} gives standard deviations of
\$0.081 and \$0.122; the bound on the standard deviation of their difference is
$\sqrt{0.081^2 + 0.122^2} = \$0.146$, so the \$1.22 gap is about 8.3 such
units. Since these are bounds, that is a statement about separation, not a tail
probability. With $\sigma_h = 0.03$ (three points of drift), the parameter
standard deviation is \$0.09 on either endpoint at $N = 200$ (it does not
depend on $h$), already comparable to the process bound; at $N = 800$ it is
\$1.44 against a process bound of \$0.63, and the ratio keeps growing as
$\sqrt{N}$.

Under \textsf{sticky} routing the picture changes: a 200-turn task starting on
Z.ai has an 89\% chance of finishing on its fallback, and its expected cost is
mostly the fallback's. The cheaper endpoint by hit rate is also the one more
likely to be abandoned.

\section{Beyond independent misses}\label{sec:beyond}

Assumption~\ref{as:indep} is the weakest link. The refinements are explicit
knobs because OpenRouter's data cannot identify them.

\paragraph{Eviction.}
Caches expire after a time-to-live $T_{\text{ttl}}$. With inter-turn gap
$\tau_k$, replace the constant hit rate by
$h_k = h \cdot \ind[\tau_k < T_{\text{ttl}}]$ or $h_k = h\, e^{-\tau_k / T_{\text{ttl}}}$.
Tool-call bursts then hit and human think-time misses.

\paragraph{Minimum cacheable length and cache cap.}
Providers publish a minimum prompt length below which nothing is cached
(1{,}024 to 4{,}096 tokens for the major closed models), so $H_k = 0$ for
$P_k$ below the threshold. If a provider caches at most $L$ tokens the cached
part of the prefix is $\min(S_k, L)$ and the remainder is billed at $m$.
Whether exceeding the cap instead invalidates the whole prefix is
provider-specific and should be a switch, not an assumption.

\paragraph{Provider state.}
The general object is a serving endpoint $Z_k$ and a cache-state vector
$\mathcal{C}_k = (C_{e,k})_e$ giving the longest prefix each endpoint holds and
when it was last touched. A transition updates only the cache of the endpoint
that successfully served the turn. Turn $k$ hits on $Z_k$ only if $C_{Z_k,k}$
covers the current prefix within its TTL. \textsf{order} and \textsf{sticky}
are the two policies for $Z_k$ that have closed forms above; session-keyed
stickiness and load-balanced routing without an order are further policies on
the same state space. The independent model is the memoryless approximation.

\paragraph{Compounding.}
If a disruption at turn $t_j$ leaves the task cold for $R_j$ turns, the
premium is $(m - r) \sum_j \sum_{k = t_j}^{t_j + R_j - 1} S_k$. With fixed
$R_j$ this is still $O(N^2)$; if recovery scales with task length it is
$O(N^3)$; \textsf{sticky} migration is the case $R_j = N - t_j$ with the
fallback's prices substituted.

\section{Reporting and implementation}

The model is implemented in \texttt{openrouter\_frontier/scoring.py}: the expectation,
decomposition, bounds, and both routing policies are evaluated turn by turn (the loop is
exact and needs no geometric-sum special cases), and the test suite checks the
implementation against the closed forms \eqref{eq:task-mean} and \eqref{eq:sticky} and
the worked example above. The default profile is the one in Section~1.4.

The unit of optimisation is the task. For each endpoint the tools should report
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\E[X_{\text{task}}]$ in dollars per completed task under the stated
        routing policy, with the decomposition \eqref{eq:decomp}: fixed, time,
        cached-read baseline, miss premium, failure premium, return penalty;
  \item the perfect-cache cost ($h = 1$) and cold-cache cost ($h = 0$) as
        bounds, and under \textsf{sticky} the probability of migrating to the
        fallback;
  \item the process bound $\sigma_{\text{proc}}$, the parameter term
        $\sigma_{\text{par}}$, and $J$;
  \item total ordinary, cache-read, cache-write, and completion tokens;
  \item the inputs $h, u, \E[\ell], \E[s]$, the cache mode and miss policy used,
        and the timestamps of the windows they summarise.
\end{itemize}
Per-million normalisation is secondary: repeatedly submitted transcript tokens
make the denominator ambiguous, and the figure falls with context while the
bill rises.
